Read papers the way a human does.

neuron7 reads your research PDFs with a local LLM and marks them up like a sharp colleague with a Sharpie: circles, underlines, questions in the margin. Or like a reviewer with a pen. Plus a TL;DR and an acronym decoder. Nothing ever leaves your computer.

How it works

From PDF to marked-up paper in three steps.

  1. 1

    Open a paper

    Drop any PDF with selectable text. A Rust backend extracts and cleans the text, finds the references, and splits it into model-sized chunks.

  2. 2

    Your local model reads it

    Gemma (or any model) running in LM Studio or Ollama annotates each chunk. Every quote is verified against the real text, so invented ones are thrown away.

  3. 3

    Read, filter, export

    Flip between Sharpie and Professional views, filter by category, delete what you disagree with, then export a summary PDF or companion notes.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is my paper really kept private?

Yes. neuron7 only talks to the model server you configure, which normally runs on localhost. There are no accounts, no analytics and no cloud calls. If you point it at a remote server, that is your choice, and the text will go there.

Do I have to use Gemma?

No. Gemma 3 works well and is the suggested default, but any chat model behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint works: LM Studio, Ollama, llama.cpp’s llama-server, Jan, KoboldCpp or vLLM. Bigger models give sharper annotations.

What hardware do I need?

It depends on the model you choose. Small models (around 4B parameters, quantised) run on most modern laptops. 12B and 27B models give noticeably better judgement but want a good GPU or plenty of RAM. neuron7 itself is light.

How long does a paper take?

That is down to your model and hardware. The paper is processed in chunks with a live progress bar, and you can cancel at any time. A failed chunk only costs you that chunk.

Can the AI make things up?

Every annotation’s quote is checked against the PDF’s own text in Rust, and anything that cannot be found is discarded. The judgement (“this looks weak”) is still the model’s opinion, so treat it like a well-read colleague’s margin notes, not ground truth. You can delete any annotation you disagree with.

Does it change my PDF?

No. Annotations are drawn on top of the page in the app. Your original file is never modified. You can export a separate Summary PDF, or print the annotated pages to a new PDF.

How does the acronym decoder work?

Definitions written in the paper, like “Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD)”, are found with pattern matching and are marked defined in paper. Only abbreviations that are never defined are sent to the model, and they are labelled inferred. When the model is not sure, it says so instead of guessing.

Does it work on scanned PDFs?

Yes. If a page has no selectable text, neuron7 runs OCR on it automatically before annotating. The OCR engine and its models are built into the app and run entirely on your computer, with nothing downloaded or uploaded. Accuracy is best on clean, upright, English or Latin-script pages; badly skewed or low-resolution scans will give rougher text.

Which platforms are supported? Why the warning on first launch?

Windows, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Linux builds are published with each release. The macOS and Linux builds are produced by GitHub Actions and have not been tested by the maintainer yet, so please report problems. The apps are not code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper show a one-time warning; the First launch tab explains how to open them.

Is it open source?

Yes, the full source is public on GitHub. Issues and pull requests are welcome.
